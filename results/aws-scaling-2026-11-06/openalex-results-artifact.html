OpenAlex benchmark · single-user resources

Same data, two paths, and one query worth fixing

Seven queries ran on nested cohorts of 2M, 10M and 31.9M works. At 31.9M, ClickHouse on EC2 was faster than Athena on six of the seven. The seventh, Topic statistics, took 65.5 s, until two separate changes brought it under 10 s in the same Docker container.

This compares two complete paths. It is not an isolated engine or hardware A/B test. ClickHouse ran in Docker at 18 CPU and 50 GiB on an m7g.8xlarge host with 32 vCPU and 128 GiB. The full corpus was not tested, and these runs do not establish minimum resources.

One input, two independent routes through AWS

Both paths began with the exact June 26 OpenAlex S3 versions. Each carried its own payload, and neither final path used a raw upload from a laptop. All 99 full typed multiset comparisons matched between the ClickHouse and Athena outputs.

As the cohort grows, Topic statistics crosses over

From the smallest cohort to the largest, works grew 15.9×. ClickHouse stayed ahead on six queries. Topic statistics grew about 100× in ClickHouse and passed Athena's time by the 10.1M cohort. The cohorts are nested and nonrandom: each larger cohort contains the smaller ones, and none is a random sample.

Topic statistics got faster without a larger container

At 31.9M works, Topic statistics (q02) was the slowest ClickHouse query and the only one behind Athena. Every arm below ran in the same 18 CPU, 50 GiB Docker container on the 32 vCPU, 128 GiB m7g.8xlarge host. What changed was the query limits, the join algorithm or the SQL.

How to read these numbers

Each path was timed under its own conditions. These definitions apply to every figure above.

The full corpus is untested.

The largest cohort is 6.25% of Works. These results set no extrapolated SLA. They do not show that the full corpus fits this setup or answers in under 60 seconds. They also do not show that 18 CPU and 50 GiB, or the 32 vCPU, 128 GiB host, is the minimum or is sufficient beyond the tested cohorts.
